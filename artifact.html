Pitch Ruler

A tuner you can check. It tracks pitch with the YIN method, names the note, and shows how many cents sharp or flat you are against a reference you choose. It listens live, analyses a recording (and estimates what A4 the recording was tuned to), or measures a virtual instrument whose true pitch it knows, so you can see for yourself how accurate it is.

Settings

Instrument range

The range sets the analysis window (three periods of its lowest pitch): a lower range is steadier on low notes but reacts more slowly and smooths fast vibrato. Strictness is the YIN threshold: lower rejects noisy frames, higher accepts them.

Method and honest notes

YIN looks for the lag at which the signal best matches a copy of itself, using a difference function normalised by its running average so that low lags are not favoured, takes the first dip below the threshold, and interpolates between samples. Here the interpolation fits a cosine through three points, which stays accurate at high pitch where the usual parabola is biased. Note names use equal temperament: semitones = 12 log2(f / A4), and cents are hundredths of a semitone.

What this is not. It is a monophonic tracker: chords and several notes at once give nonsense. The accuracy shown by the self test is on clean synthetic signals; real instruments drift, have inharmonic partials (piano, guitar bass strings) and room noise, and a real microphone or sound card can have its own sample-rate error. Nobody has checked this against a hardware tuner.

  • de Cheveigné, A., and Kawahara, H. (2002). YIN, a fundamental frequency estimator for speech and music. Journal of the Acoustical Society of America, 111(4).
  • Equal temperament and cents: standard; A4 = 440 Hz is the ISO 16 concert pitch.
